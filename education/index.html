Education

Prevention begins before a crisis becomes a case.

Educational work across student learning, open curriculum, and forensic-health settings—developed before and alongside the legislative work that became Mission 5355.

The premise

Recognition is part of prevention.

Bahadur's earliest work on sex trafficking focused not on legislation but on what people knew before they encountered a dangerous situation: how young people understood coercion, how stereotypes shaped recognition, how adults interpreted warning signs, and where misinformation created blind spots.

That work developed across three educational settings: a credit-bearing seminar for students, an open educational curriculum for broader use, and a professional guide for Sexual Assault Nurse Examiners.

The sequence matters. The legislative work grew from educational practice rather than the other way around.

Seminar · Eastside Preparatory School

Before there was a statewide curriculum requirement, there was a classroom.

Bahadur designed and institutionalized a credit-bearing seminar on sexual violence, sex-trafficking prevention, healthy relationships, and survivor-informed education during the 2022–23 academic year.

The curriculum included weekly lesson plans, readings, discussion material, and instructional resources developed with the school counselor and academic dean.

She co-facilitated two fully enrolled cohorts. The experience provided a practical setting for testing how difficult material could be taught without relying on sensationalism, stereotypes, or fear as the primary educational mechanism.

That classroom work became one of the foundations for the open curriculum and, later, the legislative model behind Mission 5355.

Open educational resource

Sexual Violence and Sex Trafficking in the 21st Century

The open curriculum expanded the classroom work into a resource designed for broader educational use.

Bahadur served as Project Lead and Grantee, with Sam Foote as Content Writer. The project received a competitive $16,000 federal/state open-education grant and was published through OER Commons.

Forensic health

Washington Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner (SANE) Guide

The educational problem changed again in a clinical setting: what happens when someone experiencing or surviving trafficking reaches a professional who may be in a position to recognize it?

Bahadur authored a professional guide for Sexual Assault Nurse Examiners addressing trafficking definitions, underreporting, stereotypes, intersectionality, and barriers in the practitioner-patient relationship. The resource was reviewed by Dr. Victoria Patricia Sattler and is currently in use at WSU College of Nursing.

From education to law

The curriculum did not end when legislation began.

Washington Senate Bill 5355 moved prevention education into state law. Mission 5355 then adapted that model across additional legislatures.

Education remains the substantive intervention underneath the legal structure: legislation determines whether and how a public system makes that learning available.
