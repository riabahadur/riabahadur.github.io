Why an open curriculum

Prevention material is only useful if people can reach it.

The curriculum grew from a credit-bearing seminar on sexual violence, trafficking prevention, healthy relationships, and survivor-informed education developed at Eastside Preparatory School.

The open-resource project moved that work beyond a single classroom. Its purpose was not to reproduce the seminar verbatim, but to create material that could be accessed, adapted, and used by educators in different instructional settings.

The project was published through OER Commons and selected as part of Washington's 2022–23 open educational resource grant program.

Subject matter

Recognition without sensationalism.

01

Sexual violence

Foundational concepts, patterns, myths, and the social context in which sexual violence is understood.

02

Sex trafficking

Definitions, trafficking dynamics, misconceptions, warning signs, and the difference between common stereotypes and lived situations.

03

Intersectionality

Attention to how race, gender, sexuality, vulnerability, and social structures can shape risk, recognition, and response.

04

Healthy relationships

Prevention through discussion of coercion, boundaries, relationships, manipulation, and the conditions that can make exploitation harder to identify.

05

Identification & response

Recognition, reporting pathways, resources, and ways students can understand when and how to seek help.

06

Advocacy

How prevention education connects to institutions, public policy, community response, and broader systems of protection.

State prevention policy

Washington SB 5355 embedded sex-trafficking awareness and prevention instruction into state law; Mission 5355 subsequently adapted the model across additional jurisdictions.

From access to implementation

The open resource widened the audience; legislation changed the delivery system.

Publishing the curriculum openly made the material available beyond the school in which it was first developed.

Washington SB 5355 addressed a different problem: whether prevention education would be offered through a statewide public-school system.
