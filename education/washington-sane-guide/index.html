Washington Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner (SANE) Guide

A professional educational resource developed to support recognition of sex trafficking in forensic-health settings.

Original guide · 2022

Recognizing existent sex-trafficking paradigms.

Washington SANE Guide: Recognizing Existent Sex Trafficking Paradigms — Mitigating Sex Trafficking, One Exam Room at a Time was authored by Ria Bahadur and reviewed by Dr. Victoria Patricia Sattler of WSU College of Nursing and the Washington Department of Health.

The guide was developed for Sexual Assault Nurse Examiners as an educational resource on trafficking recognition and is currently in use at WSU College of Nursing.

The clinical problem

A patient can reach care without being recognized as someone experiencing trafficking.

The original guide focused on the gap between a professional's intention to help and the categories through which a patient is interpreted.

It argued that stereotypes about who trafficking affects, what trafficking looks like, and how victims behave can interfere with recognition in healthcare and other victim-service settings.

The resource therefore treated trafficking recognition as more than a checklist problem. It addressed definitions, underreporting, myths, intersectionality, and barriers in the practitioner-patient relationship.

Original contents

What the 2022 guide addressed.

Statistics & underreporting

Why recorded trafficking data can fail to capture the full incidence of trafficking and how recognition affects reporting.

02

Working definitions

How definitions shape who is recognized, what professionals look for, and which experiences can disappear behind narrow categories.

03

Myths & stereotypes

Common assumptions about violence, movement, immigration, commercial sex, gender, victim behavior, and relationships with traffickers.

04

Intersectionality

How race, ethnicity, gender, sexuality, vulnerability, and social power can shape both trafficking risk and professional recognition.

05

Practitioner-patient barriers

The relationship between judgment, trust, stigma, communication, and whether a patient feels able to disclose or seek help.

06

Recognition as prevention

The role healthcare professionals can play within a broader prevention and response ecosystem.

Institutional context

WSU College of Nursing and SANE education.

WSU College of Nursing has developed Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner clinical-skills training in Washington, with Dr. Victoria Sattler among its instructors and program leaders.

The International Association of Forensic Nurses lists WSU as an approved Washington clinical-skills training site. The association also publishes current SANE Education Guidelines governing formal didactic and clinical preparation.

Bahadur's guide is a supplementary trafficking-recognition resource. It is not a substitute for current SANE education guidelines, clinical judgment, institutional protocols, licensing requirements, or jurisdiction-specific standards of care.

Archive & revision

Preserve the original. Update the medicine.

The 2022 guide is part of the historical record of the project and should remain available as the edition that was written and reviewed at that time.

Its original structure included trafficking statistics, historical and working definitions, myths, intersectionality, race and ethnicity, gender and sexuality, and practitioner-patient barriers.

A future web edition should update clinical, legal, statistical, and terminology claims against current authoritative sources rather than silently rewriting the archival document.

This distinction allows the site to show how the work evolved while keeping current educational material aligned with contemporary forensic-nursing standards.
