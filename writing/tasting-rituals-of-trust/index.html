Academic Paper · Florence · 2025

Tasting Rituals of Trust

A historical-anthropological study of Mercato Sant'Ambrogio, agricultural trade, and the social construction of trust in Florentine food culture.

The paper

Food markets make trust visible.

Written during Stanford's Florence program in spring 2025, Tasting Rituals of Trust examines Mercato Sant'Ambrogio and agricultural trade through questions of trust, authenticity, class, and globalization.

The paper treats food culture as more than consumption. Markets are places where people make judgments about provenance, quality, familiarity, legitimacy, tradition, and who or what can be believed.

The study belongs to a different subject area from Bahadur's current science-security research, but it asks a recognizably related question: how do institutions and social practices make trust legible?

Themes

Trust, authenticity, class, and globalization.

TRUST

How do consumers know?

The social cues, relationships, labels, practices, and histories through which food becomes credible.

AUTHENTICITY

What counts as local?

The relationship among place, tradition, provenance, cultural expectation, and claims of authenticity.

CLASS

Who gets access to authenticity?

How markets, consumption, pricing, and social difference shape access to particular forms of food culture.

GLOBALIZATION

What happens when the local travels?

How agricultural trade and global circulation interact with expectations of locality, tradition, and cultural identity.

Status

Academic work, not yet a public publication.
