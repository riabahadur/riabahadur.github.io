Speaking, Testimony & Public Deliberation

Some arguments are made in rooms before they are made on pages.

Research presentations, legislative testimony, rapporteur remarks, public questions, interviews, and invited scientific convenings.

Across settings

The form changes with the institution.

Legislative testimony asks whether a proposal should become law. A research presentation asks whether an argument survives scrutiny. A rapporteur has a different obligation: to represent a discussion accurately enough that people who disagreed inside the room can still recognize it afterward.

Bahadur's public speaking spans each of these forms. This page records those appearances by function rather than treating every microphone as the same kind of platform.

Earlier speaking

Competitive speech was an earlier laboratory for public argument.

Before legislative testimony and research presentations, Bahadur pioneered and captained her high school's speech program, earning more than fifty regional awards and placing third nationally in Impromptu Speaking.

That record belongs to the site's archive rather than the central professional narrative, but it helps explain an enduring part of the work: translating complex material quickly for an audience that does not share the speaker's starting assumptions.
