About Ria Bahadur

Ria Bahadur works across science, security, law, and public institutions.

Her research and public work span military wargaming, research security, legislation, education, emerging technology, and science governance.

The through line

Different fields have led back to a small set of recurring problems.

Her work spans sex-trafficking prevention, military wargaming, research security, science governance, education, and emerging technology.

A policy can succeed in one jurisdiction and fail in another. Scientific openness can enable discovery while creating difficult questions of security, competition, and trust. Strategic simulations can help institutions reason about futures they cannot observe directly.

Origins

Prevention before policy.

The earliest work began with sex-trafficking prevention at twelve, focusing on education and victim recognition.

That work developed into educational material for students and educators, followed by a professional resource for Sexual Assault Nurse Examiners that is currently in use at WSU College of Nursing.

The legislative work came later: how could prevention education move from individual programs into public-school requirements?

From prevention to law

Washington proved the proposal could become law. The next twenty-plus drafts showed how much had to change for it to travel.

At seventeen, Ria Bahadur created, drafted, and negotiated Washington Senate Bill 5355, a sex-trafficking prevention education measure that became state law in 2023, making her the youngest sole author of enacted legislation in a U.S. state.

She subsequently wrote and adapted more than twenty state-specific versions of the legislation across different legislative and implementation environments.

Early implementation results were encouraging: by 2024–25, initial estimates indicated that roughly 300 adolescents had been protected from trafficking-related situations, with that number expected to grow as statewide implementation matures.

The project later expanded toward federal and international pathways, with public records preserved where bills were formally filed or enacted.

Neuroscience

Science and neuroethics

Early laboratory research in neuroscience and biotechnology was accompanied by work in neuroethics, extending scientific questions into questions of governance and ethical limits.

UNESCO · 2024

International neurotechnology governance

At eighteen, Bahadur was invited as an Academic (Medical) Stakeholder to the consultation informing UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology.

Kyoto · 2024–

Science diplomacy and the STS Forum

Invited to the Science and Technology in Society Forum Annual Meeting at nineteen, Bahadur became one of its youngest-ever invitees and attendees. Her responsibilities later expanded from rapporteurship to formal synthesis authorship.

Earlier disciplines

The work began in more than one place.

Before her current work in science, security, law, and institutional design, Bahadur trained and competed across speech and debate, Hindustani classical music, Kathak, poetry, neuroscience, and multilingual study.

Those disciplines cultivated interests that continue to surface in her work: argument and interpretation; language and culture; performance and persuasion; and scientific inquiry.
