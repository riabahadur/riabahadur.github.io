About Ria Bahadur

Preemption across science, security, law, and human governance.

From neuroscience and trafficking prevention to research security and wargaming, Bahadur's work returns to one question: where can intervention happen earlier?

The through line

The conditions of a crisis usually exist before the crisis becomes visible.

Science and human safety accumulate over time, while institutions often encounter only the downstream snapshot: the clinical event, the exploited child, the security breach, or the strategic surprise. Bahadur's work has repeatedly moved in the opposite direction, toward the earlier signal.

Preemption, in that sense, is not only about acting sooner. It is about determining what "sooner" actually means. In neuroscience, that meant studying electrical and cellular signals before outcomes became clinically visible. In trafficking prevention, it meant education before exploitation became a case. In research security, it means identifying vulnerabilities before openness creates preventable loss. In wargaming, it means examining assumptions before they harden into plans.

The recurring institutional question is therefore not simply how to respond well, but how to redesign systems so that intervention remains possible before downstream failure.

Origins

Prevention before policy.

The earliest public work began with sex-trafficking prevention at twelve, focusing on education and victim recognition.

That work developed into educational material for students and educators, followed by a professional resource for Sexual Assault Nurse Examiners that is currently in use at WSU College of Nursing.

The legislative work came later: how could prevention education move from individual programs into public-school requirements?

From prevention to law

Washington proved the proposal could become law. The next jurisdictions showed how much had to change for it to travel.

At seventeen, Ria Bahadur created, drafted, and negotiated Washington Senate Bill 5355, a sex-trafficking prevention education measure that became state law in 2023, making her the youngest sole author of enacted legislation in a U.S. state.

The model then moved through markedly different jurisdictions. A Montana adaptation became SB 107 and law. North Dakota's HB 1569 and Nevada's SB 131 were introduced, and a Michigan version reached prefiling.

The initiative was also presented to legislators in New Mexico, Minnesota, and Massachusetts, with additional state engagement extending beyond filed bills.

Early implementation results in Washington were encouraging. By 2024–25, initial estimates indicated that roughly 300 adolescents had been protected from trafficking-related situations, with that number expected to grow as implementation matures.

International work has since become more concrete. Brunei Darussalam's Ministry of Education publicly featured Bahadur's trafficking-prevention work. In Denmark, the Secretariat of the Parliament's Children and Education Committee invited her to submit Mission 5355 materials for distribution to committee members and asked whether the materials could be made publicly available on the Parliament's website.

In the United Kingdom, correspondence with the office of Dame Diana Johnson led her office to forward Bahadur's SB 5355 briefing to MP Tonia Antoniazzi and to Kat Banyard at UK Feminista as next contacts for further discussion of a UK pathway.
