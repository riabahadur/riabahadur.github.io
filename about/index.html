The biography

A builder's route through institutions.

Bahadur is a Stanford University student pursuing Honors in Science, Technology, and Society. Her work spans military wargaming, research security, trafficking-prevention policy, neuroscience, science diplomacy, and emerging technology.

She tends to enter a problem through practice first: a lab protocol, a classroom, a legislative draft, a game, a policy room, a product workflow. The research question usually follows.

From prevention to law

Mission 5355 began with education, not legislation.

Bahadur's trafficking-prevention work began at twelve and grew through community advocacy, a credit-bearing seminar, an open educational curriculum, and a professional resource for Sexual Assault Nurse Examiners.

At seventeen, she created, drafted, and negotiated Washington Senate Bill 5355. It became law in 2023, making her the youngest sole author of enacted legislation in a U.S. state.

The model later became law in Montana and was adapted across more than 20 bills in 9+ U.S. legislatures, with additional international policy engagement in Brunei, Denmark, and the United Kingdom.

The work remains rooted in the same idea that started it: give people usable information before exploitation becomes a case file.
