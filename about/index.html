About Ria

From the laboratory to the legislature.

Ria Bahadur is a legislative author, science-security researcher, and founder. Her work asks where people and institutions can intervene before harm becomes harder to prevent.

“Innovation has always outpaced our ethical and regulatory muscle memory.”

Ria Bahadur · Sciences Po interview · 2026
Early teens

Research begins in neuroscience.

At UC Irvine’s Pathak Laboratory, Ria trained in neural stem cells, brain organogenesis, Piezo1 mechanobiology, imaging, and electrophysiology. Neurotechnology and clinical work followed.

Age 17

Washington SB 5355 becomes law.

She created, drafted, and negotiated a trafficking-prevention education measure. The Senate passed it 48–0. The House passed it 97–0. Governor Jay Inslee signed it on May 4, 2023.

Age 18

Science governance enters the picture.

Ria participated in the National Academies research-security roundtable at Hoover and, by U.S. Department of State invitation, contributed as an Academic (Medical) Stakeholder to UNESCO’s neurotechnology ethics consultation.

Age 19

Kyoto. And a new responsibility.

One of the STS Forum’s youngest-ever invitees and attendees, Ria was selected for rapporteur responsibilities. She returned in 2025 and was invited for a third consecutive Annual Meeting in 2026.

The throughline

Moving upstream.

From neural systems to trafficking prevention, research security, and wargaming, Ria returns to a practical question: where is there still room to act?

Today, that question connects her Stanford STS Honors research, work at NATO CCDCOE, Hoover’s Project Innovation, Mission 5355, and independent building.

“Science itself is just a form of innovation. What you do with it is human; whether it heals or harms depends on the choices people make. I always say, you can cut fruit with a knife or stab someone with a pencil. How do we know how someone will use those tools?”

Ria Bahadur · Sciences Po interview · 2026
