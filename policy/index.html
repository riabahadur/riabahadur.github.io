Law & Public Systems

What happens when an idea enters a public institution?

Legislative and policy work across sex-trafficking prevention, education, international technology governance, and the institutions that turn proposals into public systems.

The question

Policy is not only written. It is translated.

A proposal can survive one legislature and fail in another. Language that works in one education system can become politically or administratively impossible somewhere else. An idea can retain its purpose while its legal mechanism, implementation structure, or institutional home changes almost completely.

Bahadur's policy work began with sex-trafficking prevention education and developed through legislation, curriculum design, public testimony, interstate adaptation, and international governance.

The recurring problem is not simply how to write a policy, but how to preserve what matters when it moves.

Mission 5355

From one state law to a legislative program.

At seventeen, Ria Bahadur created, drafted, and negotiated Washington Senate Bill 5355, a sex-trafficking prevention education measure that became law in 2023, making her the youngest sole author of enacted legislation in a U.S. state.

The next question was harder: could the underlying intervention survive outside Washington?

Mission 5355 subsequently produced more than twenty adapted bills across nine or more U.S. legislatures, including a second enacted law in Montana, introduced legislation in North Dakota and Nevada, a federal pathway through 2024, and ongoing international adaptation work.

Selected legislative record

The same prevention objective took different forms in different legislatures.

Washington · 2023

Senate Bill 5355 — enacted

Washington requires schools, beginning no later than the 2025–26 school year, to offer sex-trafficking awareness and prevention instruction at least once before a student completes grade 12. The bill passed the Senate 48–0 and House 97–0 and was signed into law on May 4, 2023.

Montana · 2025

Senate Bill 107 — enacted

A Montana adaptation placed greater emphasis on statewide prevention infrastructure and the Office of Public Instruction, including educational resources, prevention efforts, school policy support, and curricula addressing child sex trafficking and human trafficking.

North Dakota · 2025

House Bill 1569 — introduced

The proposal created a formal North Dakota legislative record for human-trafficking awareness and prevention curriculum. It did not pass the House, but the process included published committee testimony and exposed a different set of political and implementation constraints.

Nevada · 2025

Senate Bill 131 — introduced

Co-authored with Successful Survivors Foundation founder Rhonda Sciortino, SB 131 proposed revisions to Nevada school curriculum relating to human trafficking. The 2025 bill did not advance; revised work is being prepared for the 2027 session.

Beyond state legislatures

Federal and international adaptation.

A federal sponsorship pathway continued through 2024. Following the 2024 election, several state and federal routes that had appeared viable narrowed or disappeared, changing where the project could productively move next.

International work has developed with legislative, diplomatic, and institutional partners in Brunei, Denmark, Japan, the United Kingdom, Spain, and the European Parliament. These are adaptation efforts rather than claims of enactment: each setting requires different decisions about curriculum, legal authority, implementation, and trafficking-prevention systems.
