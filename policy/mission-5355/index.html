Mission 5355 · Legislative Case Study

A prevention curriculum became a state law. Then the harder question began.

Mission 5355 traces the movement of a sex-trafficking prevention education model across state legislatures, a federal pathway, and international governance systems—recording what survived, what changed, what failed, and what became law.

The origin

The legislation came after the education work.

Bahadur's work on sex-trafficking prevention began years before SB 5355. It moved through student education, a credit-bearing seminar, open educational resources, and professional material for Sexual Assault Nurse Examiners before becoming a legislative proposal.

The underlying premise was simple: prevention depends in part on whether young people can recognize trafficking, grooming, coercion, stereotypes, reporting pathways, and the conditions that make exploitation difficult to identify.

Washington became the first test of whether that educational model could be embedded in a statewide public-school system.

Washington · 2023

Senate Bill 5355

At seventeen, Ria Bahadur created, drafted, and negotiated Washington Senate Bill 5355. Its enactment made her the youngest sole author of enacted legislation in a U.S. state.

Sponsored in the legislature by Senator Claire Wilson, the bill passed the Senate 48–0 and the House 97–0, returned to the Senate for concurrence, and was signed into law by Governor Jay Inslee on May 4, 2023.

LAW

Sex-trafficking awareness and prevention instruction

Beginning no later than the 2025–26 school year, Washington school districts, charter schools, and state-tribal education compact schools must offer students instruction in sex-trafficking awareness and prevention. Instruction may begin in grade seven, but each student must be offered it at least once before completing grade 12.

IMPLEMENTATION

Curriculum and professional-development infrastructure

The law directs the Office of the Superintendent of Public Instruction, subject to funding provisions, to review trafficking-prevention curricula and authorizes an account supporting curriculum and professional development.

EARLY RESULTS · 2024–2025

Approximately 300 adolescents protected in early estimates

Early implementation feedback was positive, with initial estimates indicating that roughly 300 adolescents had already been protected from trafficking-related situations by 2024–2025. Because statewide implementation continues to mature and no formal longitudinal impact study has yet been completed, this is an early estimate rather than a final impact count.

The next problem

A law cannot simply be copied into another state.

Washington established that the intervention could become law. It did not establish that Washington's language, administrative structure, coalition, or political assumptions would work elsewhere.

Mission 5355 therefore became an exercise in jurisdiction-specific adaptation. More than twenty drafts were written across nine or more U.S. legislatures, with changes to curriculum requirements, statutory placement, implementation authority, legislative framing, and the institutions responsible for carrying prevention work forward.

The result is not one model reproduced twenty times. It is a record of which parts of an intervention can move and which parts have to change.

Montana · 2025

Senate Bill 107

Montana SB 107 became the second enacted state law in the Mission 5355 legislative program.

Authored as a Montana-specific adaptation and carried by Senator Susan Webber, the bill approached prevention through the state's existing education and human-trafficking infrastructure, including the Office of Public Instruction and concerns intersecting with missing and murdered Indigenous people.

LEGISLATIVE PATH

Passed both chambers and signed

SB 107 passed the Montana Senate 42–7 on third reading and the House 90–9 on third reading. Governor Greg Gianforte signed the bill on May 5, 2025.

STATE MODEL

Prevention through statewide educational infrastructure

Montana law now directs the Office of Public Instruction to undertake activities addressing child sex trafficking and human trafficking, including educational resources, coordination, prevention efforts, school policy support, and curricula.

North Dakota · 2025

House Bill 1569

HB 1569 proposed a new section of North Dakota law relating to human-trafficking awareness and prevention curriculum. The bill received a House Education hearing and generated published committee testimony, including Bahadur's submission. It failed on second reading in the House, 25–65.

The outcome mattered analytically as well as politically: it created a formal state record for the proposal and clarified where the intervention encountered a different legislative environment.

Nevada · 2025

Senate Bill 131

Co-authored with Successful Survivors Foundation founder Rhonda Sciortino, Nevada SB 131 proposed revisions to school curriculum relating to human trafficking. It was introduced on January 30, 2025 and did not advance past the 2025 legislative deadline.

The proposal is being redrafted for the 2027 Nevada session rather than treated as a closed pathway.

Federal pathway · through 2024

Scaling changed the problem again.

Mission 5355 also developed a federal sponsorship pathway with Senator Patty Murray through 2024. A national approach raised a different set of questions from state legislation: federal authority, education governance, implementation across states, and the relationship between a national standard and locally controlled school systems.

Following the 2024 election, that pathway ended and several other routes narrowed. The project continued where viable rather than treating persistence as repetition of a strategy whose institutional conditions had changed.

International adaptation

The further the model travelled, the less useful direct replication became.

Current jurisdictions

International adaptation work has involved legislative, diplomatic, or institutional partners in Brunei, Denmark, Japan, the United Kingdom, Spain, and the European Parliament.

These entries describe active adaptation efforts and should not be read as claims that legislation has been enacted in each jurisdiction.

What changes internationally

School governance, legislative authority, national curricula, trafficking law, implementation mechanisms, terminology, and the relationship between ministries and local education systems differ substantially across jurisdictions.

International work therefore focuses on preserving the prevention objective while reconsidering the legal and institutional mechanism through which it could operate.

The record

Two enacted laws are only part of the story.

Mission 5355 includes enactments, introduced bills, failed bills, testimony, archived drafts, sponsorship pathways, educational resources, and international adaptations.

As primary records are organized for publication, this page will continue to add state-specific bill language, testimony, amendments, hearing material, media, and archived drafts.

The aim is to preserve the legislative history rather than rewrite it as a sequence of wins. Changes, failures, and abandoned routes show how the same prevention objective encounters different institutions.

That record is the substance of the project: not merely whether an idea travelled, but what happened to it when it did.
