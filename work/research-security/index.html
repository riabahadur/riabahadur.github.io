Research Security & Scientific Governance

How open can science remain when knowledge itself is strategic?

Research and institutional work across scientific openness, technological competition, international collaboration, governance, and trust.

The problem

Science depends on exchange. Security can require limits on exchange.

Modern scientific systems depend on the movement of people, information, methods, capital, equipment, and ideas across institutions and national borders.

Research-security policy sits inside the tension between preserving that exchange and protecting strategically consequential knowledge and capability.

Science policy

Research security sits inside a wider problem of scientific governance.

Bahadur's work links research security to the institutions that govern scientific openness, international collaboration, trust, and technological leadership.

The pivot · 2024

National Academies at Hoover

At eighteen, a chance meeting with Los Alamos National Laboratory director Thom Mason led to an invitation to the National Academies' Science, Technology, and Security Roundtable at the Hoover Institution.

The meeting introduced research security as a field rather than an abstract concern about scientific openness. During the public webcast, Bahadur asked a question that was answered by Rebecca Keiser, then the National Science Foundation's Chief of Research Security Strategy and Policy.

The roundtable became a pivot from laboratory science toward the governance of scientific collaboration and security. The work that followed included consultations across the National Science Foundation, Department of State, and Department of Defense.
