Science & Technology in Society Forum

Listening to science policy as it is being made.

Work across three consecutive STS Forum cycles spanning rapporteurship, selected scientific and leadership convenings, science diplomacy, research security, institutional synthesis, scientific trust, and authored conference materials.

The work

From reporting a discussion to helping shape its institutional record.

Invited to her first Annual Meeting at nineteen, Ria Bahadur is one of the youngest-ever invitees and attendees in the history of the Science and Technology in Society Forum Annual Meeting.

Her work began with rapporteurship: listening across discussions among scientific, governmental, industry, and diplomatic participants and translating those conversations into analytical summaries and formal remarks.

Over successive Forum cycles, the work expanded into research security, science diplomacy, collaboration across sectors, scientific trust, selected smaller convenings, and formal institutional synthesis.

The method

Synthesis is not transcription.

Rapporteurship requires reconstructing the structure of a discussion rather than reproducing a sequence of remarks: identifying points of agreement, unresolved tensions, competing definitions, assumptions, and questions that warrant further work.

Across the Forum sessions, that meant moving between technical, institutional, political, and diplomatic vocabularies while preserving disagreement rather than forcing consensus where none existed.

Original framework · 2026

VALID

A framework for interrogating the credibility of scientific and technical claims as they move through institutions, commercial incentives, AI systems, and information platforms.

PROVENANCE

Where did the claim come from?

People, institutions, datasets, systems, and sources.

EVIDENCE

What supports it?

Evidence, methods, assumptions, and inferential steps.

VALIDATION

What has been checked?

Review, replication, corroboration, and verification.

INCENTIVES

Who benefits?

Institutional, reputational, political, and commercial incentives.

TRANSFORMATION

What changed as it travelled?

Effects of summarization, ranking, platforms, AI, translation, repetition, and context.

Connection to the research

Trust is also an institutional design problem.

The Forum work connects directly to Bahadur's broader research on scientific governance and research security.

Questions about scientific credibility are inseparable from questions about institutions: who is authorized to evaluate a claim, how evidence is validated, what incentives shape communication, and how information changes as it passes through platforms, governments, scientific organizations, and public debate.
