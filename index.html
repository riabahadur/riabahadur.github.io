Trusted technology and human safety in an uncertain world.

Ria Bahadur studies how institutions identify and act on consequential problems before they become crises, with work spanning military wargaming, research security, legislation, education, neuroscience, and emerging technology.

The through line

Preemption before downstream failure.

Across neuroscience, trafficking prevention, research security, and wargaming, Bahadur's work asks where a problem becomes legible early enough to change its trajectory.

The setting changes, but the question does not: what signal appears before the crisis, who is positioned to recognize it, and what would an institution have to change in order to act while intervention is still possible?

LEGISLATION · 2023–

Senate Bill 5355 & Mission 5355

At seventeen, Bahadur became the youngest sole author of enacted legislation in a U.S. state when Washington Senate Bill 5355 became law in 2023. She later adapted the model across markedly different jurisdictions: SB 107 became law in Montana, HB 1569 was introduced in North Dakota, SB 131 in Nevada, a Michigan version reached prefiling, and the initiative was presented to legislators in New Mexico, Minnesota, and Massachusetts. Internationally, Brunei Darussalam's Ministry of Education publicly featured her trafficking-prevention work; the Danish Parliament's Children and Education Committee invited Mission 5355 materials for distribution to committee members; and work toward a United Kingdom pathway has continued through parliamentary and civil-society contacts.

Trajectory

Preemption has taken different forms as the setting changed.

Laboratory science trained attention on signals that precede visible outcomes. Trafficking prevention moved that instinct into education and law. Research security widened it to vulnerabilities inside scientific systems. Wargaming asks how assumptions about an uncertain future enter decisions before those assumptions harden into plans.
