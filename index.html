The law

Prevention started in a classroom before it reached a statehouse.

Bahadur's trafficking-prevention work grew from youth advocacy into a credit-bearing seminar, an internationally published open curriculum, and Washington Senate Bill 5355. She later authored Montana SB 107 and North Dakota HB 1569, co-authored Nevada SB 131, adapted the model across additional U.S. legislatures, and began carrying the framework into international policy discussions.

Science & Technology in Society Forum · Kyoto · 2024–2026

Invited at nineteen into an invitation-based international science-policy forum.

Rapporteur. Synthesis author. FUSE Fellow.

The Forum convenes senior scientific leaders, diplomats, university presidents, Nobel laureates, and technology executives. Across three Forum cycles, Bahadur moved from selected rapporteur roles to leadership convenings and formal synthesis work. In 2026, she was named a FUSE Fellow, a program recognizing emerging women leaders across science, technology, and security.

The work now

Research. Policy. Building. One recurring problem.

Across seizures, sex trafficking, emerging technology, research security, and military wargaming, the recurring questions are what institutions can see and change before failure moves downstream — and who or what legitimates the knowledge, models, and decisions that are ultimately institutionalized, implemented, and governed.
